Case Studies

A.I.D.A., Design Thinking for Elderly Drivers (Ford)

A.I.D.A. is a Ford concept designed around one goal: helping elderly drivers stay safe, independent, and genuinely happy behind the wheel, with a primary focus on the European market. It adapts to the moment, acting as an emergency responder when something goes wrong, and as a calm companion and assistant through everyday situations. The result is a system built not just to protect elderly drivers, but to support the independence they don't want to give up.

PMSE Reimagined: Subscription-Driven Digital Transformation

As part of an MBA group case study, we proposed a subscription model and digitalization strategy to help a traditional printing press survive digital disruption. My contribution was remodeling the legacy architecture into a standard digital layer built to support AI integration and the new subscription model. This layer connects the press to factories and machines, feeding real-time data back for R&D, service, and predictive maintenance. The result locks customers into continuous value through ongoing consulting, optimization, and cost reduction, rather than a one-time sale.

Nearo: Solving Germany's €28B Secondhand Delivery Gap

Nearo is a two-sided marketplace connecting buyers of bulky secondhand items with private van and transporter owners, solving the 'last mile' problem that leaves people stuck carrying furniture home on foot or paying steep mover fees. Built around students and young professionals in Germany's car-free demographic, it targets a secondhand goods market projected to nearly triple to €28.5B by 2035. As an Engineer on the team, I contributed to the platform's service architecture, connecting driver matching, pricing, and delivery tracking into one seamless booking flow.

e.Volution: From Institutional Fleets to OEM Licensing

As part of a Strategic Management group project, we conducted a VRIO analysis of e.Volution, a startup building circular, modular EVs around a long-life chassis ('Oscar') designed for decades of upgrades rather than replacement. We identified their real competitive edge in high switching costs and a 20-year asset lifecycle that legacy automakers can't easily imitate, and proposed a two-phase growth strategy: direct sales to institutional fleets like ambulances and airport shuttles first, then licensing the chassis IP to OEMs for urban logistics at scale. This structure lets e.Volution capture a defensible niche before competing head-on in the broader EV market.
